Maritime Safety Courses

Four specialized courses developed with Signal Mutual member input

OSH 2306

Maritime Safety Standards

OSHA maritime standards, shipyard competent person requirements, and regulatory compliance for marine operations.

OSH 3313

Introduction to Maritime Workers Compensation

Longshore Act fundamentals, claims management, and the intersection of safety programs with workers compensation outcomes.

OSH 3314

Special Topics in Maritime Industry

Confined space entry, hot work, crane operations, and emerging hazards specific to shipyard and terminal environments.

OSH 4314

Materials Handling in Maritime Industry

Rigging, lifting operations, cargo handling, and equipment safety for waterfront and ship repair operations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers for maritime safety professionals

The curriculum was developed with direct input from Signal Mutual member companies representing shipyards, terminals, and maritime contractors. Each course addresses specific challenges these employers identified: OSHA maritime standards, workers compensation integration, confined space and hot work operations, and materials handling. You'll graduate with knowledge that directly applies to shipyard and waterfront safety roles.
Yes. All courses are 100% online with no set class times. You access coursework when your schedule allows, whether that's between shifts, on days off, or during slower periods. Most students balance full-time employment with coursework successfully. CSU's average student age is 39, and most are working professionals.
The Maritime Safety Certificate includes four specialized courses and can be completed in approximately two terms. It's designed for professionals who want maritime-specific credentials quickly. The B.S. in Occupational Safety & Health with Maritime Concentration includes those same four courses within a complete bachelor's degree program, preparing you for management roles and CSP certification eligibility. All certificate credits transfer directly to the bachelor's program if you decide to continue.
OSH 2306 (Maritime Safety Standards) directly addresses OSHA's maritime standards including Subpart P (Marine Terminals), Subpart H (Shipyard Employment), and related regulations. The course covers competent person requirements, confined space entry procedures, hot work permitting, and other regulatory requirements specific to maritime operations.
Yes. CSU's bachelor's and master's programs in occupational safety and health are recognized by the Board of Certified Safety Professionals (BCSP) as Qualified Academic Programs. This recognition means graduates meet the educational requirements for ASP and CSP certification eligibility.
Many Signal Mutual member companies offer tuition assistance for employees pursuing safety education. CSU works with employers on direct billing and deferred payment options. Contact us to discuss arrangements that work with your employer's tuition assistance program.
